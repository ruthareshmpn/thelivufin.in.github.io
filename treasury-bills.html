Short-term sovereign security

Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

Short-term Government securities with 91-day, 182-day or 364-day maturities and no periodic coupon.

How the investment works

Buy at a discount, receive face value

91 · 182 · 364 days

T-Bills do not pay a periodic coupon. They are issued below face value and redeemed at face value, so the difference becomes the gross return when held to maturity. The quoted yield and purchase price—not a savings-account rate—determine the outcome.

Useful for

Short-term money with a known maturity date and sovereign credit exposure.

Main risk

Selling before maturity can produce a different price; reinvestment rates can also change.

Return pattern

There is normally one maturity payment rather than monthly, quarterly or annual interest.

Where to buy

Use RBI Retail Direct for auctions or market purchases

₹10,000 minimum stated by RBI

Retail Direct provides two routes: request T-Bills in the non-competitive segment of a primary auction, or buy an already-issued T-Bill in the secondary market. RBI’s FAQ states a ₹10,000 minimum. Other permitted aggregators—such as eligible banks, Primary Dealers or specified exchange channels—may also accept non-competitive bids.

Primary auction

Select the available 91-day, 182-day or 364-day T-Bill and request the desired face value without entering your own price.

Secondary market

Compare maturity date, clean market price, available quantity and implied yield before placing an order.

Settlement

RBI says securities are normally credited to the RDG account on settlement, typically one working day after the auction or trade date.

Illustrative purchase

How to buy a 182-day T-Bill

Final auction price is discovered later
  1. Log in to RBI Retail Direct. Open Primary Market and view the securities listed under Auction Watch.
  2. Select the 182-day T-Bill. Enter the face-value amount requested—₹1,00,000 in this example.
  3. Make the provisional payment. The portal may collect a markup because the weighted-average auction price is unknown when the non-competitive bid is entered.
  4. Check allotment and refund. The T-Bill is credited after settlement and any excess markup is returned to the linked bank account.

Example: ₹1,00,000 face value bought for ₹97,800

Amount paid: ₹97,800

Amount received at maturity: ₹1,00,000

Gross discount gain: ₹1,00,000 − ₹97,800 = ₹2,200

Illustrative simple annualised yield: (₹2,200 ÷ ₹97,800) × (365 ÷ 182) ≈ 4.51%

Gross maturity value: ₹1,00,000; estimated gain before tax: ₹2,200.

This is only a calculation example. Actual auction prices and yields change at every auction, and selling before maturity can produce a different result.

Early exit and borrowing

T-Bills do not have deposit preclosure

Short maturity, market-based exit

A T-Bill cannot be prematurely withdrawn like an FD. You can wait for face-value redemption on the maturity date or attempt to sell it in the secondary market before maturity. There is no stated issuer preclosure penalty, but the market price determines what you receive.

Early-exit route

Offer the T-Bill for sale through RBI Retail Direct’s secondary-market facility or another permitted intermediary.

Price movement

Short remaining maturity usually limits duration sensitivity, but prevailing yields, liquidity and days left to maturity still affect the sale price.

Alternative

If the maturity date is close and cash is not immediately required, compare the available sale quote with simply holding for face-value redemption.

Illustrative early sale

A T-Bill bought for ₹97,800 might later have a market value of ₹98,400 before maturity.

Illustrative sale gain: ₹98,400 − ₹97,800 = ₹600, before tax and any charges.

This is a market-sale result, not a fixed preclosure value.

Can you take a loan against it?

Treasury Bills are Government securities, and the Government Securities Act permits pledge, hypothecation or lien subject to applicable terms. However, retail loan availability is not automatic. The lender must accept the particular T-Bill and the way it is held, and may apply a haircut because the security matures soon.

Confirm eligibility

Ask whether the lender accepts T-Bills held in the RDG account or requires transfer to a supported demat or custodial arrangement.

Compare costs

Check interest rate, processing charges, loan-to-value, lien-marking process and whether the loan tenure fits within the T-Bill maturity.

Restriction

A pledged T-Bill generally cannot be sold freely until the lender releases the pledge or lien.

Practical caution: for a short-dated T-Bill, loan interest and processing costs may outweigh the benefit of borrowing instead of selling or waiting for maturity.

Income-tax treatment

The discount gain is not tax-free

Confirm classification
No coupon

A T-Bill has no periodic coupon, so there is no recurring interest payment like a dated G-Sec.

Maturity gain

The difference between acquisition cost and maturity proceeds is taxable. The precise income classification can depend on the instrument, transaction and whether the investor holds securities as investments or business stock.

Sale before maturity

A gain or loss on an earlier market sale is generally considered under capital-gains rules for an investment holder, subject to the applicable law and facts.

Tax caution: retain the purchase, allotment, sale and maturity statements. Because tax classification and rates can change—and the treatment can differ for traders and non-residents—confirm the return entry for the relevant year with a tax professional.
