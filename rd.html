The basics

What is a Recurring Deposit?

A Recurring Deposit is a fixed-term savings product. You agree to deposit the same amount every month, and the bank pays a fixed rate of interest. At the end of the tenure, you receive your deposits plus the accumulated interest.

Unlike a Fixed Deposit, you do not need a large lump sum on day one. That makes an RD useful for building a specific amount gradually—for example, an emergency buffer, course fee or planned purchase.

The calculation

How RD interest works

Each monthly instalment earns interest for a different length of time. Your first deposit works for almost the full tenure, while the final deposit earns interest for only a short period. Many Indian banks compound RD interest quarterly.

Formula for each instalment
Ai = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × ti
Total maturity   M = A1 + A2 + … + Am
AiMaturity value of one monthly instalment
PFixed amount deposited each month
rAnnual interest rate as a decimal; 7.1% becomes 0.071
nCompounding periods per year; quarterly means 4
tiYears that a particular instalment remains invested
MSum of all matured monthly instalments

Why every instalment is calculated separately: the first monthly deposit earns interest for longer than the last. The same compound-interest formula is applied to each deposit using its own remaining time, and the results are added to estimate the final maturity amount.

The calculator above performs that series automatically using quarterly compounding. Banks may apply deposit-date rules, rounding, penalties and tax deductions differently, so treat the result as a planning estimate and confirm the official maturity schedule before opening an RD.

Decision checklist

What to check before you open an RD

Why people choose an RD

  • Predictable monthly saving habit
  • Fixed rate for the selected tenure
  • Easy to understand and plan around

Details worth comparing

  • Penalty for missed instalments
  • Premature withdrawal rules
  • Tax on interest and post-tax return

Keep inflation in view: a predictable return is useful, but the purchasing power of your maturity amount may grow more slowly than prices. Match the product to the goal and time horizon.
