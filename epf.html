Workplace retirement saving

Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF)

A statutory retirement account for eligible employees, funded through payroll contributions and administered by EPFO.

EPFO administeredEmployer linkedTransferable UAN
Employer contribution is not always entirely EPF.

Part of the employer’s statutory contribution may go to the Employees’ Pension Scheme (EPS), subject to eligibility and wage rules. Use the EPFO passbook—not the total payroll deduction—to identify what reached EPF.

How it works

EPF essentials

Normal contribution

For most covered establishments, employee and employer contributions are commonly 12% of eligible basic wages and dearness allowance, although statutory exceptions and wage-ceiling rules apply.

Interest rate

The Government-approved EPF rate was 8.25% for FY 2024–25. The CBT recommended 8.25% for FY 2025–26; use the final EPFO notification when issued.

Job change

Transfer the balance using the UAN-linked process. Preserving service continuity can matter for tax and pension records.

Advances

Form 31 advances can be available for specified purposes including illness, housing, marriage, education and approaching retirement, subject to service and amount conditions.

Final settlement

Permitted on retirement and specified cessation or unemployment conditions. Avoid treating EPF as an ordinary demand deposit.

Nomination

Keep e-nomination, Aadhaar, PAN and bank details current so claims and survivor benefits are easier to process.

Additional employee saving

How VPF works inside EPF

Same member account

VPF is not a third account or a separate product. It is the portion you voluntarily contribute above your normal employee EPF contribution, recorded within the same provident-fund membership.

How to start

Submit the election through your employer’s payroll or HR process. The permitted percentage, enrolment window and procedure for changing or stopping it can depend on payroll policy and applicable EPFO rules.

Employer contribution

Your employer does not have to match the extra VPF amount. Its normal statutory EPF/EPS contribution continues separately.

Interest

The additional amount generally receives the same annual rate credited to the EPF member account. It is a declared annual rate, not a fixed rate locked for the full holding period.

Access

VPF normally follows EPF transfer, advance and final-settlement rules. Choosing a voluntary amount does not make that portion freely withdrawable on demand.

Tax aggregation

Mandatory employee EPF and additional VPF are counted together when applying the employee-contribution interest threshold. Review the tax impact before making a large VPF election.

Practical check: compare the higher retirement contribution with the resulting reduction in take-home pay. Keep separate emergency savings for expenses that do not qualify for an EPF advance.

Before submitting

Confirm the date of exit, bank account, Aadhaar, PAN, nomination and personal details in the member record. Resolve mismatches before filing where possible.

After settlement

Download and retain the claim acknowledgement, settlement status, passbook, Form 16A if issued and service records. These can be needed for tax reporting or future EPS claims.

VPF on closure

Because VPF is held within EPF, its balance follows the EPF transfer or settlement—it is not closed through a separate VPF form.

Avoid premature closure

Early final settlement can interrupt compounding, affect service continuity and create tax consequences. Compare transfer or an eligible advance first.

Rule caution: settlement eligibility depends on the reason and date of exit, continued employment status, age, service and the rules in force when the claim is filed. Check the current EPFO claim instructions rather than relying only on an old employer’s process.

Income tax

EPF tax overview

Employee shareSection 80C

Eligible employee contribution may count within the combined ₹1.5 lakh deduction under the old tax regime.

High contributions₹2.5 lakh threshold

Where the employer contributes, interest attributable to aggregate employee PF contributions above ₹2.5 lakh in a financial year is taxable under the prescribed method.

Employer shareCombined ₹7.5 lakh rule

Excess aggregate employer contribution to recognised PF, NPS and approved superannuation funds can become a taxable perquisite.

WithdrawalFive-year service test

Recognised PF withdrawal after five years of continuous service is generally exempt. Earlier settlement can be taxable unless an exception applies.
