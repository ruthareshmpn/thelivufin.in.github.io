Competition law and market structure

CCI Regulations

The Competition Commission of India enforces the Competition Act. It focuses on competitive markets, not on guaranteeing an individual investment, resolving an insurance claim or reversing an ordinary bank charge.

Core mandate

Three major areas of competition regulation

Economy-wide authority
Anti-competitive agreements

CCI can examine agreements or coordinated conduct that causes or is likely to cause an appreciable adverse effect on competition in India.

Cartels

Price fixing, bid rigging, output restriction or market allocation between competitors can raise serious competition concerns.

Abuse of dominance

Dominance itself is not prohibited. The issue is abusive conduct by an enterprise holding a dominant position in the relevant market.

Combinations

Qualifying acquisitions, mergers and amalgamations may need notification and review under current thresholds, exemptions and procedures.

Financial-sector relevance

CCI can overlap with sector regulators

Different questions, same group
Bank or insurer merger

Sector approvals may address prudential or licensing issues while CCI separately examines competition effects.

Digital financial platforms

Conduct involving access, tying, exclusivity or platform power can require competition analysis depending on the evidence.

Trade associations

Information exchange or coordinated market conduct can be relevant even where each member is separately licensed.

Group structures

A corporate group can face CCI review and still have entities supervised by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA.

Boundary: use RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA complaint systems for ordinary product-service disputes. Competition law addresses harm to competition, not merely dissatisfaction with one transaction.

Information and filings

Prepare facts that show the competition issue

Define the conduct and market
  1. Identify the enterprises and conduct. State who did what, when and how.
  2. Explain the market context. Describe the affected product or service, customers, geography and alternatives.
  3. Show the competition harm. Include evidence of coordination, exclusion, discriminatory conditions or other alleged effects.
  4. Use the correct statutory route. Information about conduct and combination notifications have different requirements, forms and fees.
  5. Preserve records lawfully. Keep contracts, communications, tender documents and public material supporting the allegation.
