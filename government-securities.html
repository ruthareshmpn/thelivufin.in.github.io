Sovereign fixed income

Government Securities (G-Secs)

Dated Central and State Government securities can provide coupon income and a known maturity value, while their market price can move before maturity.

How the investment works

Coupon income with market-price movement

Sovereign credit

Dated Government securities are issued by the Central Government or State Governments and normally pay a fixed coupon until maturity. Government backing addresses default risk, but it does not remove market-price risk when selling before maturity.

Return

Coupon payments plus any gain or loss between purchase price and sale or maturity value.

Main risk

Prices generally fall when market interest rates rise. Longer maturities usually move more.

Holding to maturity

The face value is repaid at maturity, subject to the security’s terms. Your actual return still depends on the price paid.

Where to buy

RBI Retail Direct and other permitted channels

₹10,000 minimum stated by RBI

Individual investors can open a free Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account with RBI and participate in non-competitive primary auctions or buy and sell securities in the secondary market. RBI’s published FAQ states a ₹10,000 minimum for dated G-Secs. Banks, Primary Dealers and permitted exchange or broker channels may also provide access, with their own account and fee arrangements.

Primary auction

Request a face-value amount without selecting your own price. Allotment uses the weighted-average price of successful competitive bids.

Secondary market

Choose an available security and place a buy order at a quoted market price through the Retail Direct secondary-market facility.

Account requirements

Rupee savings account in India, PAN, an officially valid KYC document, email address and registered mobile number.

Illustrative purchase

How to buy a dated G-Sec

Auction price can differ from face value
  1. Open and verify an RDG account. Complete PAN, bank-account and KYC verification on the official RBI Retail Direct portal.
  2. Open Primary Market and Auction Watch. Select an available dated Government security and enter the amount of face value you want to request.
  3. Fund the bid. The portal collects an indicative price plus any accrued interest and refundable markup because the final weighted-average auction price is not yet known.
  4. Receive allotment. After the auction, excess markup is refunded and the allotted security is credited to the RDG account, typically on settlement.

Example: ₹1,00,000 face value at a 7.00% annual coupon

Semi-annual coupon: ₹1,00,000 × 7.00% ÷ 2 = ₹3,500

Two coupon payments in a full year: ₹7,000

Face value repaid at maturity: ₹1,00,000

Illustrative gross cash flow over one full year: ₹7,000 coupon, before tax.

The actual auction or market price may be above or below ₹1,00,000 and accrued interest may apply. Therefore, coupon rate and your effective yield are not necessarily the same.

Early exit and borrowing

No deposit-style preclosure

Sell or pledge, subject to terms

A dated G-Sec is a marketable security, not a bank deposit. There is normally no “preclosure request” to the issuer and no fixed premature-withdrawal penalty. To exit before maturity, the investor sells the security in the secondary market at the price available then.

Early-exit route

Place a sell order through RBI Retail Direct’s secondary-market facility or another permitted intermediary. You must hold the security before offering it for sale.

Possible outcome

The sale price may be above or below your purchase price. Interest-rate movement, remaining maturity, accrued coupon and market liquidity affect the proceeds.

Not guaranteed

A buyer and acceptable quote may not be immediately available. Holding until maturity avoids having to sell at the prevailing market price.

Illustrative early sale

If ₹1,00,000 face value is sold at a clean price of ₹97.50 per ₹100, the clean consideration is approximately ₹97,500.

Accrued coupon interest may be added to the settlement amount. Taxes and any intermediary charges are separate.

The ₹2,500 clean-price difference is market movement—not a preclosure penalty.

Can you take a loan against it?

The Government Securities Act permits a holder to create a pledge, hypothecation or lien over a Government security, subject to applicable terms. That legal ability does not mean RBI Retail Direct automatically provides a retail loan. A bank or other lender must agree to accept the specific security and holding arrangement as collateral.

Ask the lender

Whether it accepts the ISIN and RDG or demat holding, its loan-to-value or haircut, loan rate, tenure, documentation and minimum amount.

While pledged

You generally cannot freely sell or transfer the security until the lender releases the lien or pledge.

Default risk

If the borrower defaults, the lender may enforce the security under the agreement. Coupon and maturity proceeds can be controlled by the lien terms.

Practical choice: compare the loan’s total interest and charges with the loss or gain from selling. Loan availability is lender-specific, so obtain written terms before relying on this option.

Income-tax treatment

Coupon income and sale gains are treated separately

Investor-specific
Coupon interest

For an investor who does not hold the security as business inventory, Government-security interest is generally reported under “Income from Other Sources” and taxed at the applicable rate.

Sale before maturity

A gain or loss on transfer is generally considered under capital-gains rules. Classification depends on factors including the holding period and whether the security is listed.

Current holding-period guide

The Income Tax Department’s current table shows listed Government securities becoming long-term after more than 12 months and unlisted Government securities after more than 24 months for transfers on or after 23 July 2024.

Tax caution: tax rates and reporting can change and may differ for traders, non-residents and special securities. Use the transaction statement and coupon records, and confirm the treatment for the relevant financial year with a tax professional.
