Securities-market regulation

SEBI Regulations

SEBI regulates India's securities market, including market institutions, registered intermediaries, public issues and listed-company disclosures. Registration authorises a category of activity; it does not guarantee investment performance.

Market structure

What SEBI regulates

Activity-specific registration
Market infrastructure

Recognised stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories operate under SEBI oversight and applicable approved rules.

Trading and custody

Stock brokers, clearing members and depository participants require the appropriate registration and institutional connection.

Investment products

Mutual funds, portfolio managers, alternative investment funds, REITs and InvITs follow separate registration, disclosure and governance frameworks.

Advice and research

Investment advisers and research analysts require their own registration categories. A broker registration does not make a person a registered adviser.

Debt-market intermediaries

Debenture trustees, credit-rating agencies, merchant bankers, registrars and online bond-platform providers have defined roles and requirements.

Listed issuers

Public issues and listed entities must follow issue, listing, disclosure and governance requirements applicable to the security and transaction.

Investor protection

Disclosure and conduct do not remove market risk

No return guarantee
Offer documents

Issuers disclose material terms and risks through prescribed documents. Investors must still assess suitability and the possibility of loss.

Continuous disclosures

Listed entities make financial, governance and material-event filings through stock exchanges. Use current filings, not promotional summaries.

Market conduct

Securities law prohibits specified manipulation, fraudulent practices and insider trading. SEBI can investigate and take action under due process.

Client assets

Brokers, depositories and other institutions follow rules for custody, settlement, statements and handling client securities and funds.

Registration boundary

A registration number applies to a legal entity and activity. It does not validate every social-media channel, employee, affiliate or recommendation using the same brand.

Product risk

Shares, bonds, funds, derivatives, REITs and InvITs have different price, credit, leverage, liquidity and operational risks.

Never treat “SEBI registered” as “SEBI approved returns.” Verify the registration and independently evaluate the investment.

Verification

Confirm the registration category and market connection

Search official records
  1. Capture the legal name and registration number. Use the agreement, website footer, contract note or offer document.
  2. Search SEBI's recognised-intermediary records. Match the name, category, registration number and status.
  3. For a broker, verify exchange membership. Confirm the member and authorised contact information on the relevant exchange.
  4. Check enforcement and cancellation records. A copied number may belong to an unrelated or inactive entity.
  5. Use exchange filings for listed securities. Verify the ISIN, issuer, offer document, financial disclosures and corporate actions.
  6. Match the payment destination. Do not send investment money to a personal account or unofficial UPI ID.

Complaint and dispute route

Entity first, then SCORES or ODR

Choose the correct channel
  1. Write to the entity. Contact the listed company or registered intermediary's grievance officer and keep the acknowledgement.
  2. Use SCORES if unresolved. Select the correct regulated entity and complaint category, upload evidence and track the Action Taken Report.
  3. Request review when applicable. SCORES provides review stages subject to its current procedure and deadlines.
  4. Use securities-market ODR for an eligible dispute. Conciliation and arbitration through SMART ODR are distinct from regulatory complaint facilitation.
  5. Report market intelligence separately. Suspected manipulation, insider trading or illegal tips may belong on SEBI's Market Intelligence portal rather than SCORES.

Common warning signs

Registration claims can be copied or misused

Verify independently
Guaranteed trading profits

Market investments can lose money. Assured high returns, loss-recovery schemes and pressure to borrow are serious warning signs.

Fake adviser identity

Fraudsters may copy a genuine adviser or broker's certificate. Match phone, email, website and payment details against official records.

Remote-access software

Do not share OTPs, passwords, PINs or screen control with someone claiming to help open or recover an investment account.

Dabba trading

Off-exchange arrangements can avoid legitimate exchange protections and are illegal. Use registered intermediaries and recognised market infrastructure.
