Liquidity with automatic deposits

What is a Sweep FD?

A sweep facility links a savings or current account to fixed deposits. Money above a chosen or bank-defined threshold moves into an FD, while a linked deposit—or only the required portion—can be broken when the transaction account needs funds.

Core ideaSave + accessterms vary by bank
Step 1Threshold

Keep an operating balance

The bank leaves money up to the sweep threshold in the transaction account. The exact threshold can depend on the account variant and minimum-balance rules.

Step 2Sweep out

Excess becomes an FD

Eligible excess funds are automatically placed into one or more fixed deposits, often in defined blocks and for a bank-selected tenure.

Step 3Sweep in

Funds return when needed

If the savings balance is insufficient, the linked FD is broken under the bank's sequence and interest rules so the required amount can return automatically.

Example · Kotak Mahindra Bank

ActivMoney two-way sweep

Kotak describes ActivMoney as automatically moving eligible balance above an account-specific threshold into a fixed deposit. Its current savings-account materials generally show a 180-day FD, with sweep-in handled on a last-in, first-out basis. Current forms commonly specify creation and breakage in ₹5,000 blocks, while some salary/current-account terms use different blocks. Regular FD rates apply; Kotak's cited two-way-sweep terms state no separate prepayment penalty. Thresholds and availability vary by account variant, and TDS applies to interest under the prevailing rules.

Where it can help

  • Automatically puts surplus transaction balance to work
  • Provides liquidity without manually closing the full FD
  • Useful for a stable buffer that regularly rises above the threshold

What to verify

  • Sweep-in/out thresholds, FD tenure and block size
  • Actual-tenure interest, break sequence and premature-closure treatment
  • Minimum-balance rules, TDS and DICGC aggregation across accounts

Income tax and TDS

Tax treatment of a Sweep FD

Bank time deposit

The automatic transfer from savings to the linked FD is not income. The interest earned on the FD portion is generally taxable at your applicable slab rate, even when it is reinvested or the deposit has not yet matured. The principal is not taxed.

Regular depositor₹50,000bank-interest TDS threshold
Senior citizen₹1,00,000bank-interest TDS threshold
Valid PAN10%standard TDS rate
PAN not provided20%generally the higher rate
  • The threshold is applied to aggregate interest credited or paid by the bank during the tax year—not separately to each sweep-created FD block.
  • If interest exceeds the applicable threshold, TDS is generally deducted on the entire interest amount, not only the amount above the threshold.
  • The savings-account deduction does not convert sweep-FD interest into savings interest: a time-deposit component remains FD interest.
  • TDS is only advance tax credit. Add the full taxable interest to your return and claim the TDS shown in Form 26AS/AIS; your final tax may be higher or lower.
  • Eligible residents expecting nil tax liability may give the prescribed self-declaration to the bank. For tax year 2026–27, the Income Tax portal identifies this as Form 121, replacing the earlier Form 15G/15H format.

Company deposit with issuer credit risk

What is a Corporate Fixed Deposit?

A corporate FD is a fixed-term deposit accepted by an eligible company under the law applicable to that issuer. It promises a stated interest rate and repayment date, but it is not a bank deposit, has no DICGC insurance, and repayment depends on the company’s financial ability.

ProtectionNo DICGCissuer-specific credit risk
Step 1Identify

Check the legal issuer

“Corporate FD” can describe a deposit from an eligible non-financial company or an RBI-authorised deposit-taking NBFC/HFC. Confirm the exact legal name and regulatory category before comparing rates.

Step 2Select

Choose cumulative or payout

A cumulative option adds interest until maturity. A payout option distributes interest monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually if offered. The stated payout rate and effective yield may differ.

Step 3Assess

Price the credit risk

A higher rate is compensation for taking issuer risk. A company can delay or fail to pay even when the deposit was lawfully issued and carried a credit rating.

Do not confuse the labels

Company deposit versus NBFC deposit

Eligible companies accepting deposits under the Companies Act and Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules are administered through the MCA framework. Deposit-taking NBFCs and HFCs require specific RBI permission. An MCA registration certificate or an RBI Certificate of Registration by itself does not prove that public deposits may be accepted.

Income tax and TDS

Tax treatment of a Corporate or NBFC FD

No special tax exemption

For a genuine company or NBFC deposit, the interest is generally taxable at your applicable slab rate under income from other sources. The deposited principal is not taxable merely because it is repaid at maturity.

Interest threshold₹10,000per payer during the tax year
Senior citizen₹10,000same “other payer” threshold
Valid PAN10%standard TDS rate
PAN not provided20%generally the higher rate
  • The ₹1,00,000 senior-citizen threshold is for interest paid by banks, qualifying co-operative banks and notified post-office deposits; it does not generally extend to an ordinary company or NBFC deposit.
  • The issuer aggregates interest across deposits held with that payer. Once the ₹10,000 threshold is exceeded, TDS is generally applied to the entire interest amount—not only the excess.
  • With a cumulative FD, tax and TDS can arise when interest is credited in the issuer’s books, even though cash is received only at maturity.
  • TDS is not the final tax. Report the full interest and claim available TDS credit; your slab-based liability may be higher, lower or nil.
  • Eligible residents expecting nil tax liability may submit the prescribed self-declaration. For tax year 2026–27, the official portal identifies it as Form 121, replacing the earlier Form 15G/15H format.
  • Confirm that the product is a deposit. Interest on an NCD or another security can fall under different tax and TDS provisions.
Simple TDS example

If one NBFC credits ₹15,000 of deposit interest during the tax year and valid PAN is available, the usual 10% TDS would be ₹1,500. You still report ₹15,000 as income and claim ₹1,500 as tax credit; this is not a calculation of your final tax.

How return works

  • Interest may be cumulative or paid periodically
  • Compounding frequency affects maturity value
  • Premature closure may reduce the applicable rate or be restricted
  • Interest is generally taxable at the investor’s applicable rate; TDS rules may apply

Key risks

  • No DICGC deposit insurance
  • Credit ratings can be downgraded and are not guarantees
  • There may be no easy secondary-market exit
  • Loan or premature-withdrawal facilities depend entirely on issuer terms
Illustrative cumulative deposit

₹1,00,000 compounded annually at a hypothetical 8% for three years grows to about ₹1,25,971 before tax. This is a mathematical illustration—not a quoted product or guarantee. Actual compounding, rounding, TDS, early-closure treatment and repayment depend on the issuer’s documents and financial position.

Checks before investing in a corporate FD

  • Verify the issuer category: determine whether it is a bank, an RBI-authorised deposit-taking NBFC/HFC, or another eligible company operating under MCA deposit rules.
  • Read the deposit circular and application: confirm tenure, rate, payout frequency, security status, nomination, renewal and repayment terms.
  • Check the current credit rating: review the rating agency, instrument, rating, outlook and date. Do not rely on an old advertisement.
  • Review financial capacity: examine audited financial statements, cash flows, debt, profitability and any reported repayment delays.
  • Understand early exit: verify lock-in, premature-withdrawal rate, penalty, loan facility and what happens after the depositor’s death.
  • Avoid concentration: do not place emergency funds or a large share of savings with one uninsured issuer merely for a higher rate.
  • Use traceable channels: pay only to the legal issuer or its authorised collection account and retain the application, receipt and maturity instructions.

The basics

What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit is a lump-sum deposit placed with a bank or other eligible issuer for a chosen period at an agreed interest rate. At maturity, a cumulative FD returns the original deposit plus accumulated interest. A payout FD distributes interest periodically instead.

Unlike an RD, which builds savings through monthly instalments, an FD starts with the full amount on day one. That makes it useful for money you already have and do not need immediately.

Choose the cash flow

Cumulative FD or interest payout?

Cumulative FD

  • Interest is reinvested until maturity
  • Benefits fully from compounding
  • Useful for a future lump-sum goal

Payout FD

  • Interest is paid monthly, quarterly or periodically
  • Can support regular income needs
  • Payouts may be discounted and do not compound in the deposit

Tax treatment

Interest is taxable even when it is reinvested

FD interest is generally included in taxable income at the applicable slab rate. For bank interest, TDS thresholds and declarations such as Form 15G or 15H may apply depending on current law and eligibility. TDS is a tax credit—not necessarily your final tax liability.
