Liquidity with automatic deposits

What is a Sweep FD?

A sweep facility links a savings or current account to fixed deposits. Money above a chosen or bank-defined threshold moves into an FD, while a linked deposit—or only the required portion—can be broken when the transaction account needs funds.

Core ideaSave + accessterms vary by bank
Step 1Threshold

Keep an operating balance

The bank leaves money up to the sweep threshold in the transaction account. The exact threshold can depend on the account variant and minimum-balance rules.

Step 2Sweep out

Excess becomes an FD

Eligible excess funds are automatically placed into one or more fixed deposits, often in defined blocks and for a bank-selected tenure.

Step 3Sweep in

Funds return when needed

If the savings balance is insufficient, the linked FD is broken under the bank's sequence and interest rules so the required amount can return automatically.

Example · Kotak Mahindra Bank

ActivMoney two-way sweep

Kotak describes ActivMoney as automatically moving eligible balance above an account-specific threshold into a fixed deposit. Its current savings-account materials generally show a 180-day FD, with sweep-in handled on a last-in, first-out basis. Current forms commonly specify creation and breakage in ₹5,000 blocks, while some salary/current-account terms use different blocks. Regular FD rates apply; Kotak's cited two-way-sweep terms state no separate prepayment penalty. Thresholds and availability vary by account variant, and TDS applies to interest under the prevailing rules.

Where it can help

  • Automatically puts surplus transaction balance to work
  • Provides liquidity without manually closing the full FD
  • Useful for a stable buffer that regularly rises above the threshold

What to verify

  • Sweep-in/out thresholds, FD tenure and block size
  • Actual-tenure interest, break sequence and premature-closure treatment
  • Minimum-balance rules, TDS and DICGC aggregation across accounts

The basics

What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit is a lump-sum deposit placed with a bank or other eligible issuer for a chosen period at an agreed interest rate. At maturity, a cumulative FD returns the original deposit plus accumulated interest. A payout FD distributes interest periodically instead.

Unlike an RD, which builds savings through monthly instalments, an FD starts with the full amount on day one. That makes it useful for money you already have and do not need immediately.

Choose the cash flow

Cumulative FD or interest payout?

Cumulative FD

  • Interest is reinvested until maturity
  • Benefits fully from compounding
  • Useful for a future lump-sum goal

Payout FD

  • Interest is paid monthly, quarterly or periodically
  • Can support regular income needs
  • Payouts may be discounted and do not compound in the deposit

Deposit protection

What DICGC covers—and what the ₹5 lakh limit means

DICGC stands for the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation, a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India. It provides deposit insurance when an insured bank is liquidated, loses its banking licence, is reconstructed or amalgamated under an applicable scheme, or comes under qualifying withdrawal restrictions.

DICGC in one lineInsurance for eligible bank deposits—not a guarantee of every investment product.
01

₹5 lakh is the total ceiling

Cover includes principal plus accrued interest, up to ₹5,00,000 for each depositor at each insured bank. If principal already equals ₹5 lakh, interest above that amount is outside the limit.

02

Accounts at one bank are combined

Savings, current, FD and RD balances across every branch of the same bank are aggregated when they are held in the same right and the same capacity.

03

Different banks are separate

The ₹5 lakh ceiling applies separately at each insured bank. Moving money between branches of the same bank does not create another insurance limit.

04

Capacity can change treatment

An individual account, a qualifying joint account, or an account held as guardian, partner or trustee can be treated separately when the legal right and capacity are genuinely different under DICGC rules.

Simple example

If you hold ₹3.5 lakh in FDs, ₹1 lakh in savings and ₹75,000 of accrued interest at the same insured bank in the same capacity, the combined balance is ₹5.25 lakh. The maximum DICGC cover is ₹5 lakh—not ₹5 lakh for each account.

Generally coveredEligible savings, current, fixed and recurring deposits maintained with an insured bank.
Not covered by DICGCNBFC or company deposits, mutual funds, shares, bonds, insurance products, crypto assets and deposits held outside India.

Always confirm that the institution appears on DICGC’s list of insured banks and read the official “same right and same capacity” rules for joint or representative accounts.

Non-banking finance companies

What an NBFC is—and why its FD carries different risk

An NBFC is a company that provides financial services such as lending, asset finance or housing finance without being a bank. Some NBFCs are permitted to accept public deposits, but RBI registration alone does not mean an NBFC may take deposits.

Important: An NBFC deposit is unsecured. It is not insured by DICGC, and RBI does not guarantee repayment. A higher offered rate is compensation for taking more issuer credit risk.

Before placing money with an NBFC

  • Verify permission: confirm it is a deposit-taking NBFC or HFC authorised to accept public deposits—not merely an RBI-registered NBFC.
  • Check the current rating: note the rating agency, rating level, outlook and date. Ratings can be downgraded and are opinions, not guarantees.
  • Read the application form: it should disclose the deposit terms, maturity, interest, premature-withdrawal rules and required regulatory declarations.
  • Review financial strength: look at profitability, capital, asset quality, liquidity, repayment history and whether deposits are overdue.
  • Keep evidence: pay through traceable banking channels and insist on an official receipt showing depositor name, amount, rate and maturity date.
  • Diversify: do not choose an issuer only because its rate is higher, and avoid concentrating emergency money in an uninsured deposit.

Verify before depositing

Official bank and NBFC directories

These official directories contain thousands of institutions and are updated by the regulators. Search the institution's full legal name on the official list immediately before investing.

Do not mix up the two lists: a bank appearing on DICGC's insured-bank list has eligible deposits covered within the applicable limit. An NBFC appearing on RBI's registered list is not DICGC-insured, and registration alone does not permit it to accept public deposits.

List of insured banks

The official searchable list covers insured commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks, local area banks and eligible co-operative banks.

  • Search using the bank's complete legal name.
  • Also check whether it has been de-registered or placed under All-Inclusive Directions.
  • Insurance is up to ₹5 lakh per depositor, per bank, in the same right and capacity, including principal and interest.

Tax treatment

Interest is taxable even when it is reinvested

FD interest is generally included in taxable income at the applicable slab rate. For bank interest, TDS thresholds and declarations such as Form 15G or 15H may apply depending on current law and eligibility. TDS is a tax credit—not necessarily your final tax liability.
