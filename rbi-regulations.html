Banking, lending and payments

RBI Regulations

The Reserve Bank of India regulates important parts of India's banking and financial system. The exact rules depend on the legal entity and activity, so verify the institution's category before relying on a regulatory claim.

Regulatory scope

Institutions and activities under RBI

Category matters
Banks

Public- and private-sector banks, foreign banks, small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks and co-operative banks operate under different permissions and supervisory arrangements.

NBFCs and housing finance

RBI registers and supervises NBFC categories under a scale-based framework. Registration alone does not permit every NBFC to accept public deposits.

Payment systems

RBI authorises payment-system operators under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act. Wallets, payment aggregators and consumer apps may involve more than one regulated entity.

Credit information

Credit Information Companies and participating lenders follow statutory and RBI requirements for credit reporting, correction and customer access.

Foreign exchange

RBI issues FEMA directions and authorisations for banks, money changers and specified remittance or foreign-exchange activities.

Government securities

RBI manages Government borrowing operations and provides Retail Direct access, while investment prices and liquidity still carry market risk.

Permissions differ

Bank, NBFC and payment-bank labels are not interchangeable

Check permitted activity
Commercial bank

Can offer banking products within its licence, including eligible deposits and credit. Confirm the bank appears in RBI's current list.

Small finance bank

A bank category focused on financial inclusion and priority-sector lending, while still operating under applicable banking rules.

Payments bank

Provides permitted deposit and payment services but does not have the same lending permissions as a universal bank.

NBFC

A financial company registered for specified activities. It is not a bank, and only eligible deposit-taking NBFCs may accept public deposits under applicable conditions.

Fintech app

“Fintech” is not itself a regulatory licence. Identify the bank, NBFC, PPI issuer or payment operator legally providing each service.

DICGC coverage

Eligible deposits at insured banks receive limited DICGC protection. NBFC deposits and investments are not covered merely because RBI regulates the entity.

Verification

Check the exact regulated entity

Use RBI records
  1. Copy the legal name. Use the agreement, sanction letter, account statement or app disclosure—not only the brand.
  2. Identify the activity. Deposit, loan, wallet, payment service and remittance permissions can belong to different entities.
  3. Search the correct RBI list. Confirm the category and current status; for an NBFC, also check whether public-deposit acceptance is shown as permitted.
  4. Match contact and payment information. Do not pay an individual's account or unknown UPI ID because a caller quotes an RBI number.
  5. Read the product terms. Regulation does not guarantee approval, repayment, interest, service quality or freedom from fraud.

Common misleading phrase: “RBI approved investment.” RBI regulation or registration of an entity is not an RBI guarantee of its product or return.

Customer protection

Rules cover conduct as well as financial soundness

Product-specific directions
KYC and customer identification

Regulated entities must follow applicable customer due-diligence and record requirements.

Fair-practice requirements

Lenders and digital-lending arrangements are subject to disclosure, conduct and grievance rules relevant to their category.

Payment security

RBI directions address authorised payment systems, authentication and handling of certain unauthorised electronic transactions.

Recovery conduct

Use of agents does not remove the regulated entity's responsibility to follow applicable recovery and customer-treatment directions.

Complaint route

Provider first, then RBI CMS when eligible

Keep written proof
  1. Complain to the regulated entity in writing. Save the complaint number, submission date and documents.
  2. Review the response period. If there is no reply within the period required by the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, or the reply is unsatisfactory, check whether the entity and issue are covered.
  3. File through RBI CMS. Select the correct entity and attach the earlier complaint and supporting evidence.
  4. Use urgent fraud channels separately. For unauthorised transactions or cybercrime, immediately secure the account, notify the provider and contact the appropriate cybercrime or police channel.
