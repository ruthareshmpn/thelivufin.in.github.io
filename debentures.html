Company debt investment

Debentures

A debenture records a company’s obligation to repay borrowed money under stated terms. Interest, maturity, security, conversion rights and investor protection vary by issue.

Core structure

Know what the company promises

Issuer credit risk

When you buy a debenture, you lend money to the issuing company rather than become an owner. The issue document determines the coupon or redemption return, payment frequency, maturity date, ranking, security and any conversion into equity.

Non-convertible

An NCD cannot convert into equity. It normally repays principal at maturity and may pay interest monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, annually or at redemption.

Partly convertible

One portion converts into equity under the stated terms, while the remaining portion continues or is redeemed as debt.

Fully convertible

The full value converts into equity at the time and price or ratio defined by the issue.

Decide before investing

Convertibility determines the maturity outcome

Cash, shares or both

Do not assume every debenture returns its full face value in cash. The offer document may require or permit conversion into equity before or on the stated conversion date. Once an amount converts, that portion normally stops being debt: future coupons on it end and the investor holds shares whose value can rise or fall.

Non-convertible debenture (NCD)

No equity conversion. A redeemable NCD normally pays the stated redemption amount in cash at maturity, subject to the issuer meeting its obligations. A perpetual issue may have no ordinary maturity date.

Partly convertible debenture (PCD)

A specified portion converts into shares. The remaining non-convertible portion is redeemed or continues as debt according to the issue terms, so the outcome can include both shares and cash.

Fully convertible debenture (FCD)

The whole convertible value becomes equity under the stated ratio or price. After full conversion there is normally no face-value principal left to redeem in cash as debt.

Optionally convertible debenture (OCD)

Conversion depends on the option stated in the documents—commonly the holder's choice, but the exact holder, issuer or other trigger must be checked. If conversion is not chosen, redemption follows the disclosed terms.

Compulsorily convertible debenture (CCD)

Conversion happens automatically on the stated date or event. The investor receives equity under the formula and generally cannot demand cash redemption of the converted amount.

Partly or fully, optional or compulsory

These labels can combine. For example, an instrument may be partly compulsorily convertible, leaving a separate non-convertible balance for redemption.

Important timing point: conversion can occur before the final maturity date. Read the conversion date or window separately from the redemption date.

Conversion checklist

Know exactly what you will receive

Read the offer document
Conversion price or ratio

Check whether each debenture converts into a fixed number of shares, at a fixed price, or through a formula linked to a future price.

Who controls the option?

Confirm whether conversion is compulsory or optional and, if optional, whether the choice belongs to the investor or another party. Note the response deadline and process.

Coupon cut-off

Identify the last coupon date and whether accrued interest is paid in cash, included in conversion or handled another way.

Cash redemption amount

For an NCD or non-convertible portion, check face value, any redemption premium, instalments and the scheduled payment date.

Resulting shares

Check the equity class, listing status, lock-in, treatment of fractional shares and adjustments for corporate actions.

Valuation risk

The share value on or after conversion can be below the debt amount converted. Conversion removes the contractual principal claim for that portion.

Example: ₹10,000 partly convertible debenture

Convertible portion: 40% × ₹10,000 = ₹4,000

Conversion price: ₹200 per share → 20 equity shares

Non-convertible portion scheduled for cash redemption: ₹6,000

Contractual maturity outcome: 20 shares plus ₹6,000 cash, before tax and only if the issuer meets the terms.

If the shares trade at ₹230 after conversion, their market value is ₹4,600; at ₹150, it is ₹3,000. This market value is not guaranteed and is separate from any coupons already received.

Protection and repayment

“Secured” does not mean risk-free

Recovery may vary

A secured debenture has a charge over identified assets for the benefit of holders, usually overseen by a debenture trustee. An unsecured debenture has no specific asset charge and relies more directly on the issuer’s ability to repay. Even with security, full recovery can depend on asset value, legal priority, enforcement time and market conditions.

Credit rating

Use the current rating as one input, not a guarantee. Check rating changes and the rationale published by the rating agency.

Debenture trustee

Check the named trustee and its disclosures about payment defaults, failure to create security and rating revisions.

Seniority

Read where the issue ranks relative to other lenders. Subordinated debt can absorb losses before senior claims.

Before investing

Coupon rate is not the whole return

Read the offer document

Compare yield to maturity, purchase price, remaining tenure and payment schedule—not only the advertised coupon. A listed NCD may trade above or below face value, and a buyer may be unable to sell quickly at a fair price.

Check the issuer

Review debt levels, cash flow, repayment history, purpose of the issue, defaults, promoter information and key risk factors.

Exit and liquidity

There is usually no bank-style premature closure. Exit normally means selling in the market, if buyers and sufficient liquidity are available.

Tax

Interest is generally taxable according to the investor’s applicable income-tax rules. Sale or redemption gains may have separate tax treatment.

Real-world listed example

IIFL Finance 2026 secured NCD

Issue closed · check live market

IIFL Finance Limited’s Tranche I public issue opened on 17 February 2026 and closed on 4 March 2026. The prospectus described secured, rated, listed, redeemable NCDs with a ₹1,000 face value. Because the public offer has closed, an interested investor would need to check whether a particular series is available in the secondary market and at what price.

Example series

The prospectus cash-flow illustration for Series VI shows a 60-month tenure, 9.00% annual coupon and stated maturity on 6 March 2031.

Ratings at issue

The prospectus recorded CRISIL AA/Stable and BWR AA+/Stable for the issue. Ratings can change and are not repayment guarantees.

Market status

“Listed” allows exchange trading but does not ensure daily liquidity. Verify the ISIN, current rating, traded price, accrued interest and order-book depth.

Illustrative cash flow for 10 NCDs bought at face value

Face-value investment: 10 × ₹1,000 = ₹10,000

Annual coupon at 9.00%: ₹10,000 × 9.00% = ₹900 per year

Five annual coupons: ₹900 × 5 = ₹4,500

Principal scheduled at maturity: ₹10,000

Illustrative total cash received by maturity: ₹14,500 before tax, if all payments are made as scheduled.

If purchased in the secondary market above or below ₹1,000 per NCD, the effective yield and gain or loss will differ. Brokerage, accrued interest and tax are not included.

Not a live-offer recommendation: the subscription window is closed. This example explains how to read an actual prospectus. Before any secondary-market purchase, confirm the current exchange quote, latest rating, issuer disclosures and exact ISIN; do not rely on the issue-date coupon or rating alone.
