Mutual Funds

A mutual fund pools investor money and invests under a stated objective. Return, volatility, liquidity and a suitable holding period depend on the selected scheme—not merely on whether the investment is made through SIP or lump sum.

How it works

Units represent your share of a pooled portfolio

Market-linked return
Asset Management Company

The AMC manages the scheme according to its objective and disclosed investment limits.

Net Asset Value

NAV is the per-unit accounting value after portfolio assets, liabilities and accrued expenses. It is not a measure of whether a fund is cheap or expensive.

Growth option

Income and gains remain invested in the scheme and are reflected in NAV, subject to market movement and costs.

IDCW option

Distributions are not assured returns. A payout generally reduces scheme NAV by the distributed amount and applicable adjustments.

SIP

A systematic investment plan invests a chosen amount periodically. It can average purchase cost but cannot guarantee profit or prevent loss.

Redemption

Open-ended units are normally redeemed using the applicable NAV rules; exit load, cut-off timing, liquidity conditions and taxes may affect proceeds.

Return

Understand what the performance number means

Past return is not assured
Absolute return

Percentage gain or loss between purchase and current/redemption value, suitable mainly for shorter single-period comparisons.

CAGR

Annualised compounded rate between a starting and ending value. It smooths the path and can hide interim volatility.

XIRR

Annualised return using the actual dates and amounts of multiple cash flows, commonly used for SIP performance.

Total return

Includes NAV movement and distributions. Compare like-for-like plans, options and periods.

Benchmark comparison

Compare the scheme with its stated benchmark and category over suitable rolling periods—not only the latest one-year ranking.

Real return

What remains after scheme costs, transaction effects, tax and inflation is more relevant to a financial goal.

Illustrative return calculation

₹1,00,000 invested at NAV ₹20 buys 5,000 units. If NAV later reaches ₹23, the holding is worth ₹1,15,000 before exit load and tax: a 15% absolute gain.

If NAV instead falls to ₹17, the holding is worth ₹85,000: a 15% capital loss. SIP instalments would each buy at a different NAV and require cash-flow-based measurement such as XIRR.

Illustration only: no return is promised, and a higher historical return normally comes with risks that must be understood.

Risk

Read the Riskometer and then inspect the portfolio

Capital can fall
Market risk

Equity, bond and commodity prices can fall because of economic, earnings, rate, currency or sentiment changes.

Credit risk

A bond issuer may be downgraded, delay payment or default, reducing debt-fund NAV and liquidity.

Interest-rate risk

Bond prices generally move opposite to yields. Longer-duration portfolios are usually more sensitive.

Concentration risk

Sector, thematic, small-cap or focused portfolios can suffer more when a narrow exposure performs poorly.

Liquidity risk

The fund may struggle to sell an asset near its quoted value during market stress; some schemes can face special liquidity measures.

Tracking risk

Passive funds may lag their index because of expenses, cash holdings, rebalancing and execution differences.

International risk

Overseas funds add currency, foreign-market, taxation, limit and geopolitical risks.

Behaviour risk

Chasing recent winners or redeeming during panic can turn volatility into permanent loss.

Riskometer: review the scheme and benchmark Riskometers in the latest factsheet. The displayed risk level can change as the portfolio and markets change.

How to invest

A practical mutual-fund investing process

Goal before scheme
  1. Define the goal and date. Separate emergency money and near-term expenses from long-term market investments.
  2. Choose an asset allocation. Decide the equity, debt and other-asset mix based on time horizon, loss capacity and existing holdings.
  3. Select a suitable category. Match the scheme objective, Riskometer and portfolio to the goal before comparing individual funds.
  4. Read current documents. Review the Scheme Information Document, Key Information Memorandum and latest factsheet, including benchmark, portfolio, expense ratio, exit load and fund-manager history.
  5. Choose direct or regular. Direct plans have no distributor commission and generally lower expenses; regular plans include distribution costs. The portfolio is normally the same, but NAV and returns differ because costs differ.
  6. Complete KYC and nominate. Invest through the AMC or an appropriate regulated/registered channel, verify bank details and retain confirmation and account statements.
  7. Choose lump sum or SIP. SIP supports disciplined cash-flow investing; it does not make an unsuitable or expensive scheme suitable.
  8. Review periodically. Rebalance for the goal, not daily market noise. Investigate persistent benchmark/category underperformance, mandate changes or rising risk.

Costs and taxation

Gross portfolio return is not your final return

Plan and holding period matter
Expense ratio

Recurring scheme expenses are reflected in NAV. Even a small annual difference compounds over long periods.

Exit load

Some schemes charge for redemption within a stated period. It is separate from tax.

Trading costs

ETF investors can also face brokerage, exchange charges, bid–ask spreads and premium/discount to NAV.

Income tax

Tax depends on the fund's portfolio classification, holding period, transaction date and current law. IDCW is not automatically tax-free.
