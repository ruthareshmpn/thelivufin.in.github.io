Income-producing infrastructure

Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs)

A SEBI-registered InvIT pools investor money to own infrastructure assets directly or through holding companies and special-purpose vehicles.

How the trust works

Infrastructure cash flows packaged into units

SEBI-regulated business trust

An InvIT raises money by issuing units and invests mainly in eligible infrastructure assets. Projects can be held directly or through a holding company or SPV. The sponsor establishes the trust, while the trustee, investment manager and project manager have separate responsibilities.

Underlying assets

Roads, power transmission, renewable-energy assets, telecom towers or fibre, pipelines, ports, airports, logistics and other notified infrastructure sectors.

How value reaches investors

Operating assets can generate toll, availability, transmission, lease or contracted revenue that may support distributions to unit holders.

What a unit represents

A beneficial interest in the trust—not a bank deposit, fixed coupon or direct title to a specific road, tower or transmission line.

Types of InvIT

Public, privately listed and private unlisted trusts

Access and liquidity differ
Public listed InvIT

Units are initially offered to the public and traded on a recognised exchange. This is the most accessible structure for ordinary retail investors, subject to broker, demat and market availability.

Privately placed listed InvIT

Units begin through a private placement to eligible investors and are subsequently listed. Entry conditions and trading lots can make access less retail-friendly.

Private unlisted InvIT

Units are privately placed and are not exchange traded. Access, transfer, valuation and exit depend on the placement terms and eligible-investor rules.

Important: “listed” describes where units may trade; it does not guarantee a buyer, stable price or easy exit. Check the offer document or placement memorandum for the exact structure.

Infrastructure types explained

How each asset earns—and where it can go wrong

Project-specific exposure

Roads and highways

Toll projects collect from traffic, so vehicle growth, competing routes and toll revisions matter. Annuity and HAM projects receive scheduled authority payments, reducing traffic risk but adding authority-payment and performance risk. TOT concessions involve an upfront concession payment for the right to collect tolls for a defined period. Across all models, remaining concession life, maintenance obligations and debt are central.

Power transmission

Transmission lines and substations often earn availability-based tariffs under long-term frameworks. Stable availability can support predictable cash flow, while outages, delayed projects, regulation, grid curtailment and payment delays from utilities can reduce it.

Renewable energy and storage

Solar, wind and battery assets may earn under long-term power-purchase or storage agreements. Review the buyer’s credit quality, tariff, contract life and escalation. Sun or wind variability, equipment degradation, curtailment, land issues and battery replacement costs remain real risks.

Telecom towers and fibre

Towers earn tenancy or site rentals; fibre networks earn capacity and lease revenue. Tenancy ratio, contract tenure, escalation and customer concentration drive returns. Operator consolidation, renewals, technology changes and high upgrade capex can affect distributions.

Pipelines and energy infrastructure

Gas or petroleum pipelines, terminals and related assets may earn regulated tariffs or contracted capacity charges. Throughput, ship-or-pay protection, customer concentration, safety, environmental compliance and tariff resets determine cash-flow resilience.

Warehousing and logistics parks

Grade-A warehouses earn rent from logistics, manufacturing, retail and e-commerce occupiers. Occupancy, rent escalation, lease expiry, tenant concentration and access to transport corridors matter. New supply and specialised-fitout costs can affect renewal economics.

Ports, airports and transport hubs

Revenue can include user charges, cargo or passenger fees, leases and commercial income. Economic cycles, concession terms, regulation, competing hubs, weather disruption and continuing capital expenditure can make cash flows more variable.

Diversified portfolios

Holding many projects, regions or revenue models can reduce dependence on one asset, but does not remove leverage, refinancing, manager, valuation or related-party risk. Examine concentration by asset, customer, authority and debt maturity—not only the number of projects.

How a retail investor can invest

  1. Confirm that units are publicly available. SEBI registration alone does not mean a trust is open to retail purchase; check its offer document and current exchange status.
  2. Use a demat and trading account. Publicly traded units can be bought through the exchange at the available market price.
  3. Read the distribution breakdown. Interest, dividend, debt repayment and other components can have different tax treatment.
  4. Monitor the assets and debt. Track availability, counterparties, refinancing, acquisitions, valuations and related-party transactions.

Return components

Distribution yield is not a guaranteed interest rate

Cash flow + unit price

An investor’s total return combines cash distributions received and the change in market value of the units. The distribution can contain several components with different tax treatment.

Interest

Interest received by the trust from an SPV and passed through to unit holders can form part of the distribution.

Dividend

Dividend received from an SPV may be distributed. Its tax treatment depends partly on the SPV’s tax regime.

Debt repayment

A distribution may be labelled repayment of shareholder debt or another non-pass-through amount. It is not automatically tax-free.

Capital appreciation

Units may trade above or below the investor’s purchase price because of yields, asset performance, interest rates and demand.

Capital return

Asset sales or restructuring may change distributions and NAV. A large payout does not by itself mean recurring operating income increased.

Yield calculation

Annual cash distribution ÷ current unit price gives a trailing yield, not a promise of next year’s distribution.

Illustrative one-year return

100 units purchased at ₹120 = ₹12,000 invested

Cash distributions during the year: ₹9 per unit = ₹900

Year-end market price: ₹125; unrealised price gain = ₹500

Illustrative total before tax: ₹1,400, or 11.67% of the initial ₹12,000.

If the price instead ended at ₹110, the ₹1,000 price decline would more than offset the ₹900 distribution. Actual distributions and prices can vary.

Principal risks

Look through the trust to the projects and debt

Capital and income can vary
Operating risk

Traffic, plant availability, generation, utilisation, maintenance and outages can change project cash flow.

Counterparty risk

Government agencies, utilities, tenants or concession authorities may delay or dispute payments.

Leverage and refinancing

Debt can amplify returns and losses. Higher rates or difficult refinancing can reduce distributions.

Regulatory risk

Tariffs, concession rules, environmental conditions, taxes and sector regulation can change.

Concentration risk

A trust concentrated in one road, region, customer or sector is more exposed to a single adverse event.

Valuation and liquidity

Infrastructure valuation uses assumptions, while exchange trading volume may be limited when an investor wants to sell.

Income-tax treatment

Read the distribution statement component by component

Business-trust taxation
Interest distribution

Pass-through interest from the SPV is generally taxable to a resident unit holder at the applicable rate. Section 194LBA generally provides 10% TDS for relevant payments to residents.

Dividend distribution

Taxability can depend on whether the SPV has chosen the corporate-tax option under section 115BAA. Do not treat every InvIT dividend as automatically exempt.

Debt-repayment component

Non-pass-through amounts can be taxed under the statutory formula for business-trust distributions after considering the issue price and earlier amounts. Keep the cumulative distribution record.

Sale within 12 months

For an exchange sale of listed business-trust units subject to STT, current official guidance shows short-term gains taxed at 20% for transfers on or after 23 July 2024.

Sale after 12 months

Listed business-trust units generally become long-term after more than 12 months. Section 112A currently provides 12.5% tax on aggregate eligible long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh, subject to its conditions.

Use Form 64B

The trust provides a component-wise distribution statement. Match it with broker records, TDS certificates and the annual information statement.

Tax caution: this is a resident-individual overview. Non-residents, business holdings, off-market transfers and future law changes can produce different results. Use the trust’s Form 64B and consult a tax professional for the relevant year.
