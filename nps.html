Retirement investing

National Pension System (NPS)

Build a long-term retirement corpus through PFRDA-regulated pension funds. Returns depend on market performance, your contributions, asset mix, charges and time invested.

Govt backed frameworkMarket linkedPortable PRAN
Government-regulated does not mean guaranteed.

NPS operates under the Government’s pension framework and is regulated by PFRDA, but PFRDA states that it provides no implicit or explicit assurance of benefits. Your corpus can rise or fall with the underlying investments.

How it works

NPS details

Who can join

The current PFRDA All Citizen page permits eligible Indian citizens, resident or non-resident, and OCIs aged 18–85, subject to KYC. HUFs and PIOs are not eligible.

Tier I

The primary retirement account. Withdrawals and exit are governed by NPS rules, and eligible tax benefits may apply.

Tier II

A voluntary investment account with easier access. It generally does not receive the same tax treatment as Tier I; limited exceptions may apply.

Investment choices

Choose active or lifecycle allocation across equity, corporate debt, Government securities and permitted alternatives. Returns and risk vary with the mix.

Portability

Your Permanent Retirement Account Number—PRAN—can continue across jobs and locations, subject to the applicable sector and account rules.

Costs and records

NPS has account, transaction, fund-management and annuity-related charges. Review the current PFRDA and CRA disclosures before enrolling.

Access and retirement

Withdrawals and exit choices

Normal exitLump sum plus annuity

Current PFRDA rules provide different choices by model, joining age and corpus. A commonly used tax-aligned illustration is up to 60% lump sum with at least 40% used for annuity; some current operational options permit up to 80% lump sum with at least 20% annuity.

Premature exitNormally 20% lump sum

Before the applicable normal-exit point, the standard rule generally permits up to 20% as lump sum and requires at least 80% for annuity, subject to small-corpus and other current exceptions.

Partial withdrawalUp to 25% of own contributions

Partial withdrawals may be available for specified purposes and subject to contribution-period, frequency, interval and documentation conditions. This is not 25% of the total market value.

Important: Exit regulations changed in 2026. Verify your sector, model, joining age, corpus threshold and latest PFRDA rules before acting.

Income-tax treatment

How NPS is taxed

Tax treatment depends on who contributes, the tax regime selected and how money is withdrawn. The calculator does not reduce returns for tax.

Your contributionSections 80CCD(1) and 80CCD(1B)

Under the eligible old-regime framework, your contribution can fall within the combined ₹1.5 lakh limit, with an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B). The same contribution cannot be claimed twice.

Employer contributionSection 80CCD(2)

An eligible employer contribution may receive a separate deduction within salary-based limits. Current Income Tax guidance provides up to 14% for government employees and, under the new regime, up to 14% for other eligible employees.

Growth inside NPSNo annual tax on fund growth

Investment gains accumulating within the NPS account are not taxed each year in the subscriber’s hands.

Final lump sumUp to 60% of corpus is tax-exempt

Tax law currently exempts final withdrawal up to 60% of the total corpus. If an operational exit option permits a larger lump sum, do not assume the amount above 60% receives the same exemption.

Annuity purchase and pensionPurchase is exempt; pension is taxable

The amount applied to buy the annuity is not taxed at purchase, but pension or annuity payments are taxable in the year received at the applicable rate.

Partial withdrawal and deathSpecific exemptions apply

Qualifying partial withdrawals are exempt up to the statutory limit, and the amount received by a nominee on the subscriber’s death is fully exempt under current guidance.

Tax note: The old and new tax regimes treat contribution deductions differently. Verify the law for the relevant financial year and keep PRAN contribution and exit records.
