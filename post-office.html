Compounding and long-term growth

Schemes designed to build a maturity value

Monthly contributions

5-year Recurring Deposit · 6.70%

Each instalment earns for a different length of time. Interest is compounded quarterly, so the first deposit grows longer than the last.

Example: ₹1,000 each month means ₹60,000 deposited; estimated maturity is about ₹71,366.

Single deposit

National Savings Certificate · 7.70%

Interest compounds annually and is paid with the principal after five years. The rate is locked when the certificate account is opened.

Example: ₹1,00,000 × (1.077)5 ≈ ₹1,44,903.

Long-term account

Public Provident Fund · 7.10%

Interest is calculated monthly on the lowest balance between the close of the fifth day and month-end, then credited at financial year-end. Future rates can change quarterly.

Example: ₹1 lakh eligible for every month of one year earns about ₹7,100 if 7.10% remains unchanged.

Girl-child savings

Sukanya Samriddhi Account · 8.20%

It uses monthly balance calculations and annual credit, with a Government rate that may change. Deposits are allowed for 15 years and normal maturity is after 21 years.

Example: ₹1 lakh eligible for a full year earns about ₹8,200 if 8.20% remains unchanged.

Doubling certificate

Kisan Vikas Patra · 7.50%

The published rate corresponds to a fixed 115-month doubling period for new accounts in this quarter. Returns accumulate rather than paying regular income.

Example: ₹1 lakh matures to ₹2 lakh after 9 years and 7 months.

Flexible access

Post Office Savings Account · 4.00%

Interest is based on qualifying monthly balances and credited annually. It is a transaction savings account, not a locked compounding certificate.

Example: a steady eligible ₹1 lakh balance for one year earns about ₹4,000.

Regular-interest products

Fixed payouts versus compounding

Annual payout

Time Deposit · 6.90% to 7.50%

Available rates are 6.90% for one year, 7.00% for two years, 7.10% for three years and 7.50% for five years. Interest is calculated quarterly and payable annually.

₹1 lakh examples: about ₹7,081, ₹7,186, ₹7,291 or ₹7,714 per year respectively.

Monthly payout

Monthly Income Account · 7.40%

The annual rate is divided into monthly payments. The payout does not earn further MIS interest, while the original principal remains until maturity.

Example: ₹1 lakh × 7.40% ÷ 12 ≈ ₹617 a month.

Quarterly payout

Senior Citizens Savings Scheme · 8.20%

Available only to eligible applicants under the scheme rules. Interest is paid quarterly and does not compound within the SCSS account.

Example: ₹1 lakh × 8.20% ÷ 4 = ₹2,050 each quarter.

How the examples are calculated

Three different interest treatments

Compound maturity

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

Used as an illustration for products such as NSC. P is principal, r is the annual rate, n is compounding frequency and t is years.

Periodic income

Payout = P × r ÷ k

Used for MIS or SCSS, where k is 12 monthly or 4 quarterly payouts. The principal itself does not grow from those paid-out amounts.

Recurring deposits

M = A₁ + A₂ + … + A₆₀

Every monthly RD instalment is compounded for its own remaining period; the matured instalments are then added together.

No longer open to new deposits

Special schemes are not part of the current comparison

Mahila Samman Savings Certificate: new accounts stopped after 31 March 2025. Existing accounts continue under their original two-year, 7.50% quarterly-compounded terms. PM CARES for Children is a beneficiary-specific programme rather than a generally available savings product.

Do not confuse the institutions

IPPB is a payments bank—not the scheme issuer

RBI-licensed payments bank

India Post Payments Bank

Payments banks may provide savings, payments and remittance services, but RBI guidance says they cannot accept recurring or fixed deposits. IPPB can facilitate payments toward Department of Posts products; the National Savings schemes on this page remain Government small-savings products.
