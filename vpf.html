Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)

An EPF member’s additional employee contribution above the normal statutory share—credited to the same provident-fund account.

Not a separate accountNo required employer matchEPF-linked rate
Voluntary contribution does not mean flexible withdrawal.

How it works

VPF essentials

Eligibility

You generally need to be an active EPF member and opt through your employer’s payroll process.

Contribution level

You may elect an amount above the statutory employee share, subject to eligible salary, payroll controls and EPFO rules. Your take-home salary falls by the extra deduction.

Employer match

The employer is not required to match the additional VPF contribution; its normal statutory contribution continues under applicable rules.

Return

VPF is credited in the EPF account and generally receives the same annual rate. Rates are declared, not locked for the full investment period.

Changing VPF

Employers may allow elections only at specified times or require written payroll instructions. Check when an increase, decrease or stop request becomes effective.

Access

EPF advances and settlement conditions apply. VPF should therefore be treated as retirement-oriented money, not an emergency savings account.

Tax and suitability

What to check before increasing VPF

Section 80CDeduction cap is shared

Eligible VPF contributions can count under the old regime’s combined ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C limit, together with EPF, PPF and other eligible items.

Taxable interest₹2.5 lakh aggregate threshold

When the employer contributes, interest related to aggregate employee PF contributions above ₹2.5 lakh for the year is taxable. Mandatory EPF and VPF are counted together.

LiquidityLower take-home pay

Only choose an amount sustainable after emergency savings, insurance and near-term expenses are covered.

ConcentrationBalance the portfolio

VPF offers a declared provident-fund return but does not replace diversification across suitable growth and liquid assets.
